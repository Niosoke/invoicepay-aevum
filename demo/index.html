InvoicePay

Stablecoin invoices on the QDay Aevum testnet (chain 44005). The payer first approves a purchase order on chain: which merchant may bill it, the total cap, and an expiry. The merchant then creates invoices against that approval, in full or in parts, and the contract rejects anything beyond the approved total. Every invoice also names the milestone it bills, and a milestone can only be billed once per purchase order: a resubmitted bill for work already paid is rejected even while budget remains, while the next installment with the same amount goes through. The named payer settles each invoice on chain and the contract marks it paid. The contract never holds funds: payment goes straight from payer to merchant.

How this demo works

  1. Connect a wallet. The demo adds the QDay Aevum testnet for you.
  2. As the payer, approve a purchase order: name the merchant, the total cap, and an expiry.
  3. As the merchant (switch account in your wallet), create an invoice against that purchase order and name the milestone it bills.
  4. Try the guided check below: approve, bill two equal installments, watch a resubmitted milestone get rejected, then pay both.
  5. As the payer, open your invoice view, verify each invoice and its milestone against your approval, pay, and copy the settlement proof for your records.

You need testnet tQDAY for gas and tUSD8 to pay with. Both come free from the faucet at faucet.qday.info. This is a testnet: nothing here has real value.

1. Connect

The contract address below is the live deployment. You only need to change it if you deploy your own copy.

2. Payer: approve a purchase order

You are the payer. Approve the purchase order before the merchant bills you: name the one merchant allowed to bill it, the total cap in tUSD8, and how many days the approval stays open for new invoices. The purchase order text is hashed before it goes on chain. Invoices can settle the cap in parts, but never beyond it. An approval that has not been used yet can be replaced by approving again; once an invoice reserves against it, its terms are locked.

3. Merchant: create an invoice

You are the merchant. The payer must have approved this purchase order for your address first (step 2). Name the payer, the amount, a due date, a memo, the purchase order this invoice settles, and the milestone it bills. The amount can be part of the approved total, for example one delivery of several. The milestone names the piece of work being billed, for example "PO 3001 milestone 1": each milestone can be billed only once per purchase order, so a resubmitted bill is rejected while the next installment goes through. The contract rejects the invoice if there is no approval, if the approval names another merchant, if it has expired, if the milestone was already billed, or if the amount would push the total past the approved cap.

The memo is public: it is stored on chain and emitted in the invoice event, so keep it fictional or generic. The purchase order reference and the milestone reference are different: only their hashes go on chain, never the plain text.

Guided check: two equal installments settle, a resubmitted milestone is rejected

This walks through the milestone promise of InvoicePay in five steps. It uses its own purchase order reference below. Use a fresh purchase order text (for example PO 4001) so it does not collide with an approval you already made. Steps A and E run as the payer, steps B, C and D as the merchant, so switch accounts in your wallet between steps. The two installments carry the identical amount on purpose: what tells them apart is the milestone each one names. The memos in this check are neutral labels on purpose: a memo is public on chain, while the purchase order and milestone references are stored only as hashes.

Step A. As the payer, approve this purchase order for the merchant above, capped at the approved total.

Step B. Switch to the merchant account. Create the first installment invoice, naming milestone 1 of this purchase order.

Step C. Still as the merchant, create the second installment for the same amount, naming milestone 2. Same amount, different milestone: this one is legitimate and must be accepted.

Step D. Still as the merchant, try to bill milestone 1 again for the same amount. Budget still remains under the cap, but the milestone is already claimed, so the contract must reject it. A rejection here is the demo working as intended.

Step E. Switch back to the payer account and pay both installments. Each settles exactly once, and both milestones stay locked afterwards.

4. Payer: invoices addressed to you

Switch to the payer account, then load your invoices. For each one the demo reads your purchase order approval and the milestone claim from the contract so you can verify the merchant, the amount, the milestone it bills, and how much of the approved total is already settled before you pay. After payment you get a settlement proof you can copy into your records.

5. Invoices on chain

Read live from the contract. Refresh after creating or paying an invoice. The PO column shows the short form of the purchase order hash bound to each invoice, and the Milestone column shows the short form of the milestone hash it bills.
