InvoicePay

Stablecoin invoices on the QDay Aevum testnet (chain 44005). A merchant creates an invoice in tUSD8 bound to a purchase order, the named payer settles the exact amount on chain, and the contract marks it paid. One purchase order can only be settled once: a second invoice for the same purchase order is rejected. The contract never holds funds: payment goes straight from payer to merchant.

How this demo works

  1. Connect a wallet. The demo adds the QDay Aevum testnet for you.
  2. As the merchant, create an invoice naming the payer, the amount, and the purchase order it settles.
  3. Try the guided check below: create a second invoice for the same purchase order and watch the contract reject it.
  4. As the payer (switch account in your wallet), approve tUSD8 and pay the original invoice.
  5. Watch the invoice list below flip from Open to Paid, with explorer links for every transaction.

You need testnet tQDAY for gas and tUSD8 to pay with. Both come free from the faucet at faucet.qday.info. This is a testnet: nothing here has real value.

1. Connect

The contract address below is the live deployment. You only need to change it if you deploy your own copy.

2. Merchant: create an invoice

You are the merchant. Name the one wallet that is allowed to pay this invoice, set the amount in tUSD8, pick a due date, add a memo your payer will recognise, and give the purchase order this invoice settles. The purchase order text is hashed before it goes on chain. Only one live invoice per purchase order is allowed: a second invoice for the same purchase order is rejected until the first is cancelled.

Guided check: one purchase order, settled once

This walks through the core promise of InvoicePay in three steps. It uses the payer address and amount from step 2 above, and its own purchase order reference below. Use a fresh purchase order text (for example PO 2001) so it does not collide with an invoice you already created.

Step A. As the merchant, create the invoice for this purchase order.

Step B. Still as the merchant, try to create a second invoice for the same purchase order. The contract must reject it. A rejection here is the demo working as intended.

Step C. Switch to the payer account in your wallet, then pay the original invoice. It settles exactly once, and the purchase order stays locked afterwards.

3. Payer: pay an invoice

Switch to the payer account in your wallet first. Paying takes two wallet confirmations: one to approve the contract to spend the invoice amount of tUSD8, then one to pay. Only the named payer can pay, and only once.

4. Invoices on chain

Read live from the contract. Refresh after creating or paying an invoice. The PO column shows the short form of the purchase order hash bound to each invoice.
