InvoicePay

Stablecoin invoices on the QDay Aevum testnet (chain 44005). The payer first approves a purchase order on chain: which merchant may bill it, the total cap, and an expiry. The merchant then creates invoices against that approval, in full or in parts, and the contract rejects anything beyond the approved total. The named payer settles each invoice on chain and the contract marks it paid. The contract never holds funds: payment goes straight from payer to merchant.

How this demo works

  1. Connect a wallet. The demo adds the QDay Aevum testnet for you.
  2. As the payer, approve a purchase order: name the merchant, the total cap, and an expiry.
  3. As the merchant (switch account in your wallet), create an invoice against that purchase order.
  4. Try the guided check below: approve, create, watch an over the cap invoice get rejected, then pay.
  5. As the payer, open your invoice view, verify each invoice against your approval, pay, and copy the settlement proof for your records.

You need testnet tQDAY for gas and tUSD8 to pay with. Both come free from the faucet at faucet.qday.info. This is a testnet: nothing here has real value.

1. Connect

The contract address below is the live deployment. You only need to change it if you deploy your own copy.

2. Payer: approve a purchase order

You are the payer. Approve the purchase order before the merchant bills you: name the one merchant allowed to bill it, the total cap in tUSD8, and how many days the approval stays open for new invoices. The purchase order text is hashed before it goes on chain. Invoices can settle the cap in parts, but never beyond it. An approval that has not been used yet can be replaced by approving again; once an invoice reserves against it, its terms are locked.

3. Merchant: create an invoice

You are the merchant. The payer must have approved this purchase order for your address first (step 2). Name the payer, the amount, a due date, a memo, and the purchase order this invoice settles. The amount can be part of the approved total, for example one delivery of several. The contract rejects the invoice if there is no approval, if the approval names another merchant, if it has expired, or if the amount would push the total past the approved cap.

Guided check: approved once, settled up to the cap, never beyond

This walks through the core promise of InvoicePay in four steps. It uses its own purchase order reference below. Use a fresh purchase order text (for example PO 4001) so it does not collide with an approval you already made. Steps A and D run as the payer, steps B and C as the merchant, so switch accounts in your wallet between steps.

Step A. As the payer, approve this purchase order for the merchant above, capped at the approved total.

Step B. Switch to the merchant account. Create an invoice for the full approved total against this purchase order.

Step C. Still as the merchant, try to create another invoice against the same purchase order. The approved total is already fully reserved, so the contract must reject it. A rejection here is the demo working as intended.

Step D. Switch back to the payer account and pay the original invoice. It settles exactly once, and the purchase order stays locked afterwards.

4. Payer: invoices addressed to you

Switch to the payer account, then load your invoices. For each one the demo reads your purchase order approval from the contract so you can verify the merchant, the amount, and how much of the approved total is already settled before you pay. After payment you get a settlement proof you can copy into your records.

5. Invoices on chain

Read live from the contract. Refresh after creating or paying an invoice. The PO column shows the short form of the purchase order hash bound to each invoice.
